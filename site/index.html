    Price comparison

    Every pick starts with a price, not a story. We compare the prices you can actually bet on exchanges (Novig, ProphetX, Kalshi) with Pinnacle, the sharpest sportsbook, after removing Pinnacle's built-in margin. The working assumption is that the market is right; a pick needs the available price to be clearly better than Pinnacle's fair price, not a reason to like the player.

    Pinnacle's fair price

    A sportsbook's two prices on a market add up to more than 100% (the margin, or vig). Removing that margin gives a fair, no-margin probability for each side. For NFL game-day picks and college football, Pinnacle alone is the reference (no consensus fallback since Oct 31). The mid-week props model prices each play twice, once against Pinnacle and once against Kalshi, and flags plays where both agree.

    Shin's method for removing the margin

    Since Oct 26 the margin is removed with Shin's method, which puts more of the margin on the underdog. Spreading it evenly made big underdogs look likelier than they are. The same method is used for the fair price at posting and for the closing price used to grade closing-line value.

    Exchanges and fees

    The price on each pick is the price available on that venue at posting. Break-even is calculated after the venue's fee where the venue charges one (ProphetX takes a cut of winnings; Kalshi charges a trading fee), so a pick's edge is what's left after costs. Each open pick shows the worst price still worth taking on that venue.

    Only venues available in California are used for picks: exchanges and prediction markets (Novig, ProphetX, Kalshi, DraftKings Predictions, FanDuel's prediction market) and pick'em apps. Their legal status can change; check what's legal where you are.

    Pick thresholds

    A game-day pick is posted only when the price you can get is at least 3% better than Pinnacle's fair price and the fair chance of winning is at least 2 points above the price's break-even (the 2-point minimum since Oct 28; smaller edges mostly disappeared by kickoff). Strong picks are larger edges and get a bigger stake (see Units).

    Timing

    Most picks go out on Sunday mornings, right after inactives are announced, when our research showed prices are softest. Breaking quarterback and injury news triggers extra checks during the week (see the News wire). Since Oct 28, Saturday preview edges are posted as paper only: two weeks of tracking showed they were gone by Sunday's close.

    Picks appear on this site 20 minutes after they are logged. Each is committed to the public repository when it appears, before kickoff.

    Closing-line value

    Closing-line value (CLV) is how much better our price was than the fair closing price, in points of win probability. If our price needed a 48% chance to break even and Pinnacle's no-margin price at kickoff said 51%, the pick beat the close by 3 points. Beating the close consistently is a better guide to long-run results than a few weeks of wins and losses, which are mostly noise below several hundred bets.

    The headline average counts only picks where Pinnacle quoted the same line at the close and its price changed after we posted. When the line moved, the close is estimated; when the price never changed, the number just repeats our starting edge. Those picks show a value, marked est. or flat, but are left out of the average. The all-picks figure is shown as reference only.

    Ranges shown as "95% range" are uncertainty intervals: for NFL CLV, the average plus or minus about two standard errors; for the NBA tests, a bootstrap that resamples whole games. A range that includes zero means the data can't yet tell the result apart from no edge.

    Grading

    Results come from official box scores. A player who doesn't play is void and counts as zero. Every pick is graded the same way and counted in the record. NFL picks are graded against Pinnacle's close; NBA picks are graded against Kalshi's price at tip-off, because Pinnacle's NBA prop prices rarely move before the game.

    Real money and paper

    Every pick is posted before kickoff at a real, available price, graded the same way, and counted in the record. A strategy goes on Real money once it has at least 30 graded picks and a return on stakes above +10%; it goes back to Paper if its return falls below +10%. Each pick keeps the label it had when it was posted. A strategy is retired if its pre-registered test fails, or after 100 graded picks with a return below −10% and negative closing-line value; its picks stay in the record.

    Return at a few dozen picks is mostly luck, so real money is not a claim that a strategy is proven. Closing-line value is shown alongside as the better guide.

    An outside review and our own recheck found that NFL closing-line value was smaller than it looked. Counting only closing lines Pinnacle actually moved, it was about zero over the first 26 measurable picks, so rules v1 was retired on Oct 31. Rules v2 passes only if 150 measurable picks beat Pinnacle's close by at least 1 point, with the 95% range above zero, and also beat Kalshi's close. That is judged once, by Jan 13, 2027.

    Units and staking

    Every stake and result is in units, so the record means the same thing whatever your bankroll. One unit is 2% of your bankroll. With a $500 bankroll, 1 unit is $10: a 0.5u pick is $5, a 0.75u Strong pick is $7.50, and a +1.2u week would be up $12. Picks are flat 0.5u to 0.75u, and long shots (+150 or longer) stay at 0.5u. Stakes never go up after losses.

    Teasers

    Two-team, six-point teasers on underdogs of +1.5 to +2.5 in games with totals of 49 or less. Teasing through 3 and 7 has won about 78% of legs since 2014, above the 74% needed at the usual price. Teasers have no closing-line value, so they are judged on results. On paper since Nov 2, like everything else.

    NBA research

    NBA picks are on paper while we test them. Every bet the rules would make is posted, graded and counted on the NBA research page. Rules v3.1 (since Oct 28) needs an exchange price (Novig or ProphetX) whose break-even, after fees, is at least 3 points below Kalshi's price for the same side, with no limit per game or per night. To pass, 400 graded v3.1 picks must win at least 3 points more often than their break-even, with the 95% range above zero and a profit, judged by Feb 15, 2027 at the latest. The separate matchup test is decided at 1,500 bets or Jan 31, 2027.

      Limitations

      • The samples are small. A few hundred bets are needed before wins and losses mean much, and the closing-line averages here rest on dozens of picks, not hundreds.
      • A positive record doesn't prove an edge, and a negative one doesn't disprove it. That's why each test has a fixed sample size and pass mark.
      • Closing-line value can only be measured cleanly when Pinnacle quotes the same line at the close and its price moves. Many picks don't meet that bar and are left out of the average.
      • The prices shown were available at posting. Prices move, limits are small on exchanges, and you may not get the same number.
      • Paper results are not bets. They show what the rules would have done at the posted price.
      • The news wire shows what the watcher picked up and when. It doesn't show which item led to which pick.
